\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work presented the Retrix pipeline for source retrieval over scientific web claims. The implemented system follows the four stages described in the paper: French and German queries are translated into English, queries are expanded with \texttt{BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5}, candidate papers are retrieved with a BGE-M3 hybrid retriever that combines dense, sparse, and ColBERT-style signals, and the candidate lists are reranked with neural cross-encoders. The system operates at document level, representing each paper through its title and abstract for the hybrid retrieval stage and plus the authors for the reranking stage, returning ranked \texttt{pubkey}s for evaluation.
The results support three main conclusions. 

First, analyzer and BM25 tuning provide a useful lexical baseline, with the best analyzer-focused run reaching 0.503 MRR@5 and 0.796 Recall@100 in the English run analysis. 

Second, moving to BGE-M3 hybrid retrieval produces a stronger candidate generator: the retained configuration, \texttt{bge\_m3\_hybrid\_513}, reaches 0.557 MRR@5 and 0.839 Recall@100.

Third, reranking the hybrid candidates with \texttt{nvidia/llama-nemotron-rerank-1b-v2} gives the largest observed improvement among the reported single-stage runs, reaching 0.702 MRR@5 and 0.911 Recall@100 when reranking the top 1000 candidates. Alternative rerankers such as Qwen3-reranker-4B and \texttt{Alibaba-NLP/gte-reranker-modernbert-base} also improve over retrieval alone, but remain below the Nemotron runs in the reported MRR@5 values.

The development results show that fine-tuning the Nemotron reranker is promising but should still be interpreted carefully. On the English development split, the fine-tuned Nemotron LoRA run reaches 0.716 MRR@5 and 0.913 Recall@100, compared with 0.692 MRR@5 and 0.909 Recall@100 for the non-fine-tuned top-1000 baseline. The corresponding fine-tuned development runs reach 0.750 MRR@5 for French and 0.655 MRR@5 for German. Since these runs are reported separately by language and no aggregate multilingual score or statistical validation, we treat them as language-specific evidence rather than as a single overall result.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
The main limitation of the current evaluation is that the neural stages are computationally demanding: the documented pipeline relies on CUDA-capable hardware for BGE-M3 encoding and cross-encoder reranking, which makes latency and candidate-depth trade-offs an important practical concern.

Future work should therefore focus on a more systematic validation of the current pipeline. First, the reranking and retrieval comparisons should be complemented with paired statistical tests and per-language error analysis. Second, the candidate depth used for Nemotron reranking should be studied together with runtime and memory costs, since deeper reranking improves Recall@100 and MRR@5 in the available runs but increases the amount of neural scoring required. Third, the existing fine-tuning code for rerankers and retrievers should be evaluated more systematically with hard negatives. Finally, the translation and query-expansion stages should be analyzed per language, since the current results suggest that the same pipeline can be applied across English, French, and German but do not yet isolate where cross-lingual errors enter the ranking process.
