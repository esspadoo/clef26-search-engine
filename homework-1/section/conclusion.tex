\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This project explored a practical, reproducible pipeline for document retrieval built around three complementary stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Query expansion: LLM-based and PRF-style;
    \item High-recall candidate generation via a hybrid BGE-M3 retriever that fuses dense, sparse and ColBERT-like signals; 
    \item Neural reranking where a cross-encoder (a fine-tuned version of \texttt{nvidia/llama-nemotron-rerank-1b-v2}) is applied to refine the top candidates.
\end{enumerate}

Our empirical findings and practical experience can be summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Neural reranking yields the largest single improvement in ranked quality: applying a cross-encoder to a high-recall candidate set substantially increases MRR@5 and precision at small cutoffs compared to lexical baselines alone. This improvement is even more noticeable if the re-ranking model is fine-tuned.
    \item A hybrid retrieval approach (dense + sparse + ColBERT) produces a robust candidate set that improves downstream re-ranker performance, reducing significantly the inference time to produce the re-ranked results while maintaining good averall performance. Careful weighting of the signals and the use of a candidate multiplier were important to maintain recall.

    \item Traditional analyzer and BM25 tuning remain valuable: they provide a competitive, computationally cheap baseline and act as a useful fallback when GPU resources are limited.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Limitations and future work}
The experiments reported in this repository are exploratory and optimized for research reproducibility rather than for production latency. The lack of suitable hardware components has hindered the project's development, imposing time constraints and limitations on the size of the models used. Looking ahead there are several areas for future development. First of all a first fine-tuning of the embedding model used and a better one for the re-ranker model would certainly increase the performance of the system. The opportunity to have the datasets of the last year CLEF \cite{checkthat_lab_clef2025} that were very similar to the ones of this year avoided us to use techniques as data augmentation but it would be interesting to discover if they could have improve our system's performance. A more in-depth studying of hard-negatives techniques for the fine-tuning of the re-ranker would also be interesting such as "Hard Negative Mining for Domain-Specific Retrieval in Enterprise Systems"\cite{meghwani2025hardnegativeminingdomainspecific} and others.
