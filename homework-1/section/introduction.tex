\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

To avoid fake news and online misinformation spread through platforms like Twitter, Facebook and Instagram is really important to be able to map the social media claims to a scientific paper that can provide certified and reliable information. This challenge is exemplified by Task 1 of the CLEF 2026 CheckThat! Lab where participants have the task to match each post to the correct scientific paper from a fixed pool of candidate documents. 

The proposed solution adopts a three-stage hybrid information retrieval architecture.

The second stage generates an initial list of candidates based on dense similarity using a bi-encoder model (e.g. BGE-m3) to encode both queries and documents into dense vector representations by taking advantage of a FAISS index \cite{faiss_documentation}. For each query it enriches this candidate set by computing additional sparse lexical matching scores and ColBERT-style late interaction scores. The final ranking is computed as a weighted linear combination of these three scores (dense, sparse and multi-vector), with configurable weights and the top-k documents are returned.


The third stage refines the top results by applying neural re-ranking algorithms.
